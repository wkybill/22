## 中文摘要对比

### 原摘要

全球海洋生态系统正面临暖化、酸化、脱氧和层化加剧等多重胁迫，识别不同生态层级的气候变化信号何时超过自然变率，并比较其时间和空间分布差异，是理解人为气候变化影响海洋生态系统的关键。本文利用 CESM2-LE 10个集合成员及 CESM2-FEISTY 鱼类生态模型，划分环境变量、低营养级变量和鱼类功能群变量，按超过基准期集合成员均值 ± 2倍标准差且持续5年的标准计算出现时间（Time of Emergence，ToE）。结果显示，上层温度上升ToE范围最广，主要在2000-2020年；底层温度上升ToE由于水团交换略晚于上层温度，但大西洋底层因噪声低反而出现更早上升ToE；硝酸盐和硅酸盐下降ToE集中于高纬海区，分别多在2020-2040年和2000-2020年；铁浓度在南大洋、赤道太平洋等海区升高，在北冰洋、北大西洋等海区降低，ToE多在2040-2060年。低营养级变量受较强自然变率影响，ToE多推迟至2040-2060年，且分布更破碎；鱼类功能群变量中远洋鱼下降ToE最早，部分海区早于1980年，饵料鱼下降ToE多在2000-2020年，底栖鱼受近岸高噪声影响尚未识别到ToE。研究表明，全球海洋生态系统气候变化信号中低营养级变量ToE晚于环境变量，鱼类功能群变量受营养级放大效应ToE会早于低营养级变量，但近岸生态系统会因高噪声而滞后。

**关键词：**海洋生态系统；气候变化信号；出现时间

### 修改后摘要

全球海洋生态系统正受到暖化、酸化、脱氧和层化增强等多重压力影响。不同生态层级的气候变化信号何时能从自然变率中被识别，是判断人为气候变化生态影响及其传播过程的重要问题。本文基于 CESM2-LE 10 个集合成员和 CESM2-FEISTY 鱼类生态模型，选取环境变量、低营养级变量和鱼类功能群变量，以 1920—1949 年为基准期，按集合平均值超过基准期均值 ±2 倍标准差且持续 5 年的标准计算出现时间（Time of Emergence，ToE），并结合信噪比解释区域差异。结果显示，温度信号出现最早，上层 150 m 温度上升 ToE 主要集中在 2000—2020 年，北大西洋部分底层海区因自然变率较低可在 1980 年前识别到升温信号；硝酸盐下降 ToE 多见于 2020—2040 年，硅酸盐下降 ToE 主要集中在 2000—2020 年，铁浓度 ToE 多出现在 2040—2060 年。低营养级变量的 ToE 整体晚于环境变量，空间分布更破碎：净初级生产力在高纬和部分铁限制海区增加，小型浮游植物与硅藻呈现竞争关系，中型浮游动物变化与硅藻较一致，底层颗粒有机碳通量主要反映近岸生产力变化。鱼类功能群整体呈下降趋势，其中浮泳鱼下降 ToE 最早，部分海区早于 1980 年；饵料鱼下降 ToE 多在 2000—2020 年；底栖鱼受近岸高自然变率影响，可识别范围较小。研究表明，气候变化信号在海洋生态系统中并非同步出现，而是沿环境背景、低营养级和鱼类功能群逐级传递；食物供给减少、升温压力和栖息地变化会放大高营养级响应，这为理解气候变化对海洋生态系统的长期影响提供了依据。

**关键词：**海洋生态系统；气候变化信号；出现时间；营养级放大；CESM2-FEISTY

## English Abstract Comparison

### Original Abstract

Global marine ecosystems are facing multiple stressors, including warming, acidification, deoxygenation, and enhanced stratification. Identifying when climate change signals at different ecological levels exceed natural variability, and comparing their temporal and spatial distribution patterns, is critical for understanding the impacts of anthropogenic climate change on marine ecosystems. In this study, 10 ensemble members from CESM2-LE and the CESM2-FEISTY fish ecological model were used to classify variables into environmental variables, lower-trophic-level variables, and fish functional-group variables. Time of Emergence (ToE) was calculated based on the criterion that a signal exceeded the baseline-period ensemble-member mean by ± 2 standard deviations and persisted for five consecutive years. The results show that the ToE of increasing upper-ocean temperature has the widest spatial extent, mainly occurring during 2000–2020. The ToE of increasing bottom temperature is slightly later than that of upper-ocean temperature due to water-mass exchange; however, earlier ToE of bottom-temperature increase occurs in the Atlantic Ocean because of lower noise. The ToE of decreasing nitrate and silicate concentrations is mainly concentrated in high-latitude seas, occurring mostly during 2020–2040 and 2000–2020, respectively. Iron concentrations increase in the Southern Ocean, equatorial Pacific, and other regions, while decreasing in the Arctic Ocean, North Atlantic, and other regions, with ToE mostly occurring during 2040–2060. Lower-trophic-level variables are strongly affected by natural variability, with ToE mostly delayed until 2040–2060 and showing more fragmented spatial distributions. Among fish functional groups, the ToE of declining pelagic fish occurs earliest, before 1980 in some regions. The ToE of declining forage fish mostly occurs during 2000–2020, whereas no ToE is identified for demersal fish due to high nearshore noise. This study indicates that, among climate change signals in global marine ecosystems, the ToE of lower-trophic-level variables generally lags behind that of environmental variables. Due to trophic amplification effects, fish functional-group variables may show earlier ToE than lower-trophic-level variables, while coastal ecosystems may experience delayed emergence because of high noise.

**Keywords:** marine ecosystem; climate change signal; Time of Emergence

### Revised Abstract

Marine ecosystems are exposed to warming, acidification, deoxygenation, and increasing stratification. Identifying when climate change signals at different ecological levels become distinguishable from natural variability is important for understanding how anthropogenic climate change affects marine ecosystems and propagates through food webs. This study uses 10 CESM2-LE ensemble members and the CESM2-FEISTY fish model to analyze environmental variables, lower-trophic-level variables, and fish functional groups. Taking 1920–1949 as the baseline period, the Time of Emergence (ToE) is defined as the first year when the ensemble mean exceeds the baseline mean by ±2 standard deviations for at least five consecutive years, and signal-to-noise ratios are used to interpret regional differences. The results show that temperature signals emerge earliest. The ToE of increasing upper-150 m temperature is mainly concentrated in 2000–2020, while bottom warming in parts of the North Atlantic can be detected before 1980 because of lower natural variability. The ToE of nitrate decline mostly appears in 2020–2040, silicate decline mainly in 2000–2020, and iron concentration changes mostly in 2040–2060. Lower-trophic-level variables emerge later than environmental variables and show more fragmented spatial patterns: net primary production increases in high-latitude and some iron-limited regions, small phytoplankton and diatoms show competitive changes, mesozooplankton follows diatom patterns more closely, and bottom particulate organic carbon flux mainly reflects coastal productivity. Fish functional groups generally decline, with pelagic fish showing the earliest declining ToE, before 1980 in some areas; forage fish declines mostly emerge during 2000–2020; and demersal fish show limited detectable ToE because of high nearshore variability. These findings suggest that climate change signals do not appear simultaneously across marine ecosystems, but are transmitted from environmental drivers to lower trophic levels and then to fish functional groups. Reduced food supply, warming pressure, and habitat changes may amplify high-trophic-level responses, providing a basis for understanding the long-term ecological effects of climate change in the ocean.

**Keywords:** marine ecosystem; climate change signal; Time of Emergence; trophic amplification; CESM2-FEISTY
