## 中文摘要对比

### 原摘要

全球海洋生态系统正面临暖化、酸化、脱氧和层化加剧等多重胁迫，识别不同生态层级的气候变化信号何时超过自然变率，并比较其时间和空间分布差异，是理解人为气候变化影响海洋生态系统的关键。本文利用 CESM2-LE 10个集合成员及 CESM2-FEISTY 鱼类生态模型，划分环境变量、低营养级变量和鱼类功能群变量，按超过基准期集合成员均值 ± 2倍标准差且持续5年的标准计算出现时间（Time of Emergence，ToE）。结果显示，上层温度上升ToE范围最广，主要在2000-2020年；底层温度上升ToE由于水团交换略晚于上层温度，但大西洋底层因噪声低反而出现更早上升ToE；硝酸盐和硅酸盐下降ToE集中于高纬海区，分别多在2020-2040年和2000-2020年；铁浓度在南大洋、赤道太平洋等海区升高，在北冰洋、北大西洋等海区降低，ToE多在2040-2060年。低营养级变量受较强自然变率影响，ToE多推迟至2040-2060年，且分布更破碎；鱼类功能群变量中远洋鱼下降ToE最早，部分海区早于1980年，饵料鱼下降ToE多在2000-2020年，底栖鱼受近岸高噪声影响尚未识别到ToE。研究表明，全球海洋生态系统气候变化信号中低营养级变量ToE晚于环境变量，鱼类功能群变量受营养级放大效应ToE会早于低营养级变量，但近岸生态系统会因高噪声而滞后。

**关键词：**海洋生态系统；气候变化信号；出现时间

### 修改后摘要

全球气候变化正在持续改变海洋的物理化学环境，并通过营养盐供给、初级生产和食物网过程进一步影响海洋生态系统。为了识别不同生态层级中气候变化信号何时能够从自然变率中稳定显现，本文基于 CESM2-LE 10 个集合成员和 CESM2-FEISTY 鱼类生态模型，选取环境变量、低营养级变量和鱼类功能群变量，采用基准期均值 ±2 倍标准差并持续超过 5 年的标准计算气候变化信号出现时间（Time of Emergence，ToE），并结合信噪比分析其空间差异。结果表明，环境变量中海水温度信号最早、范围最广，上层 150 m 平均温度的上升 ToE 主要出现在 2000—2020 年，底层温度整体响应较晚，但北大西洋底层因自然变率较低和深层水团输送影响，在部分海区更早识别到升温信号。营养盐变化具有明显区域性，硝酸盐和硅酸盐下降 ToE 主要集中在北冰洋、南大洋和北大西洋高纬海区，铁浓度则在南大洋、赤道太平洋等海区升高，在北冰洋、北大西洋等海区降低，多数区域 ToE 出现在 2040—2060 年。低营养级变量的响应比环境变量更复杂：净初级生产力在高纬海区和部分铁限制海区增加，小型浮游植物与硅藻呈现一定竞争关系，中型浮游动物变化与硅藻较为一致，底层颗粒有机碳通量则主要体现近岸生产力对底层生态系统的影响。由于这些变量受生态过程和自然变率共同调控，其 ToE 整体晚于温度变量，空间分布也更加破碎。鱼类功能群均表现出不同程度的潜在生物量下降，但出现时间和空间格局存在差异：浮泳鱼受食物来源减少和升温压力共同影响，下降信号最早；饵料鱼主要受中型浮游动物和上层热环境控制，下降 ToE 多见于 2000—2020 年；底栖鱼更多受底层颗粒有机碳输入和底层温度影响，在近岸高自然变率背景下可识别 ToE 的范围有限。总体来看，全球海洋生态系统气候变化信号具有明显的层级传递特征，环境变量首先响应外部强迫，低营养级变量在生态过程调节下表现出更强区域差异，而鱼类功能群则因食物供给、温度和栖息地变化的共同作用呈现营养级放大效应。

**关键词：**海洋生态系统；气候变化信号；出现时间；营养级放大；CESM2-FEISTY

## English Abstract Comparison

### Original Abstract

Global marine ecosystems are facing multiple stressors, including warming, acidification, deoxygenation, and enhanced stratification. Identifying when climate change signals at different ecological levels exceed natural variability, and comparing their temporal and spatial distribution patterns, is critical for understanding the impacts of anthropogenic climate change on marine ecosystems. In this study, 10 ensemble members from CESM2-LE and the CESM2-FEISTY fish ecological model were used to classify variables into environmental variables, lower-trophic-level variables, and fish functional-group variables. Time of Emergence (ToE) was calculated based on the criterion that a signal exceeded the baseline-period ensemble-member mean by ± 2 standard deviations and persisted for five consecutive years. The results show that the ToE of increasing upper-ocean temperature has the widest spatial extent, mainly occurring during 2000–2020. The ToE of increasing bottom temperature is slightly later than that of upper-ocean temperature due to water-mass exchange; however, earlier ToE of bottom-temperature increase occurs in the Atlantic Ocean because of lower noise. The ToE of decreasing nitrate and silicate concentrations is mainly concentrated in high-latitude seas, occurring mostly during 2020–2040 and 2000–2020, respectively. Iron concentrations increase in the Southern Ocean, equatorial Pacific, and other regions, while decreasing in the Arctic Ocean, North Atlantic, and other regions, with ToE mostly occurring during 2040–2060. Lower-trophic-level variables are strongly affected by natural variability, with ToE mostly delayed until 2040–2060 and showing more fragmented spatial distributions. Among fish functional groups, the ToE of declining pelagic fish occurs earliest, before 1980 in some regions. The ToE of declining forage fish mostly occurs during 2000–2020, whereas no ToE is identified for demersal fish due to high nearshore noise. This study indicates that, among climate change signals in global marine ecosystems, the ToE of lower-trophic-level variables generally lags behind that of environmental variables. Due to trophic amplification effects, fish functional-group variables may show earlier ToE than lower-trophic-level variables, while coastal ecosystems may experience delayed emergence because of high noise.

**Keywords:** marine ecosystem; climate change signal; Time of Emergence

### Revised Abstract

Global climate change is reshaping the physical and chemical conditions of the ocean and further affecting marine ecosystems through nutrient supply, primary production, and food-web processes. To identify when climate change signals at different ecological levels emerge from natural variability, this study uses 10 ensemble members from CESM2-LE together with the CESM2-FEISTY fish model. Environmental variables, lower-trophic-level variables, and fish functional-group variables were analyzed, and the Time of Emergence (ToE) was defined as the first year when the ensemble mean exceeded the baseline mean by ±2 standard deviations and remained beyond the threshold for at least five consecutive years. Signal-to-noise ratios were also used to interpret regional differences. The results show that temperature signals emerge earliest and cover the broadest area among the environmental variables. The ToE of increasing upper-150 m ocean temperature mainly occurs during 2000–2020, while bottom-temperature signals generally appear later. In parts of the North Atlantic, however, bottom warming emerges earlier because of low natural variability and heat transport associated with deep-water formation. Nutrient changes show strong regional patterns. The ToE of declining nitrate and silicate is mainly found in the Arctic Ocean, Southern Ocean, and high-latitude North Atlantic. Iron concentrations increase in the Southern Ocean and equatorial Pacific but decrease in the Arctic Ocean and North Atlantic, with ToE in most regions occurring during 2040–2060. Responses of lower-trophic-level variables are more complex than those of environmental variables. Net primary production increases in high-latitude regions and some iron-limited areas; small phytoplankton and diatoms show contrasting patterns related to community competition; mesozooplankton changes are closely linked to diatom biomass; and bottom particulate organic carbon flux mainly reflects the influence of coastal productivity on benthic ecosystems. Because these variables are jointly regulated by ecological processes and internal variability, their ToE is generally later than that of temperature and more spatially fragmented. Fish functional groups all show declining potential biomass to varying degrees, but their timing and spatial patterns differ. Pelagic fish respond earliest under combined pressure from reduced food supply and warming, forage fish are mainly controlled by mesozooplankton and upper-ocean thermal conditions, and demersal fish are more closely related to bottom particulate organic carbon flux and bottom temperature, with limited detectable ToE in nearshore high-variability regions. Overall, climate change signals in global marine ecosystems show a clear hierarchical transmission pattern: environmental variables respond first to external forcing, lower-trophic-level variables develop stronger regional differences under ecological regulation, and fish functional groups display amplified responses driven by changes in food supply, temperature, and habitat conditions.

**Keywords:** marine ecosystem; climate change signal; Time of Emergence; trophic amplification; CESM2-FEISTY
