# English Abstract

Global marine ecosystems are facing multiple stressors, including warming, acidification, deoxygenation, and enhanced stratification. Identifying when climate-change signals at different ecological levels exceed natural variability is essential for understanding the impacts of anthropogenic climate change on marine ecosystems.

Based on 10 ensemble members from CESM2-LE and CESM2-FEISTY outputs, this study examines environmental variables, lower-trophic-level variables, and fish functional-group variables. The Time of Emergence (ToE) is defined as the first year in which the ensemble mean exceeds the baseline-period mean by ±2 standard deviations for five consecutive years, and regional differences are interpreted using the signal-to-noise ratio.

The results show that temperature exhibits the most robust signal among the environmental variables. The ToE of increasing upper-150 m ocean temperature had already emerged across broad ocean regions around 2000. Bottom-temperature ToE occurs later than surface-layer ToE in the Southern Ocean, the Arctic Ocean, and western boundary regions of the Pacific Ocean, whereas the ToE of increasing bottom temperature had already emerged in the North Atlantic before 1980. Among nutrients, the ToE of declining nitrate is mainly concentrated in 2020–2040, while that of silicate is mainly concentrated in 2000–2020. Iron concentrations show no clear long-term global trend, with ToE in most ocean regions occurring during 2040–2060.

ToE for lower-trophic-level variables emerges later and displays more spatially fragmented patterns, generally occurring during 2040–2060. Small phytoplankton and diatoms exhibit a competitive relationship, while mesozooplankton changes are relatively consistent with diatom changes. The global annual mean time series of all three fish functional groups decline. The ToE of declining pelagic fish occurs earliest, before 1980 in some regions. The ToE of declining forage fish is more widespread and mainly occurs during 2000–2020. Demersal fish show the latest declining trend, with ToE limited to regions such as the Indian Ocean, the Arctic Ocean, and the Antarctic continental margin.

This study demonstrates a clear trophic-level hierarchy in the propagation of climate-change signals through global marine ecosystems, providing new insights into how different ecological levels respond to climate forcing.

**Keywords:** marine ecosystem; climate-change signal; Time of Emergence; CESM2-FEISTY
